How a trip works

From the moment she presses SOS to the moment she walks into the facility.

  1. She asks for help

    She presses SOS and chooses the facility she wants to go to, usually the one where she attends antenatal care. Her location is shared if she allows it. If not, she can type where she is or ask the rider to call her. She can also record a few seconds saying what is wrong.

  2. The nearest rider is called

    Approved riders close to her get a call on their phone, a few at a time, nearest first. The first to accept is shown the way to her, and she sees who is coming, their phone number and vehicle. If nobody accepts, the zone manager is alerted to find someone.

  3. The facility gets ready

    Once a rider accepts, the facility sees who is coming, how far away she is and her voice note, and marks that they are ready. If they have no bed or no doctor on duty, they send her on to another facility and the rider's route changes.

  4. She arrives

    At the facility she gives the rider a short code from her phone, which confirms she was dropped off. The ride is then paid. Her family can follow the trip from a link if she shares it.

What it costs

The app is free. She pays only for the ride, after she has arrived.

  • Cash, mobile money or card. Whatever she has to hand.
  • Chango funds. Where a community committee runs a fund, it can cover rides for the women it has enrolled.
  • Set per district. The zone manager agrees the fare for their district.

Joining MAlert

Four kinds of people make a trip happen.

Pregnant women

Download the app, sign up with your own phone number and choose a 4-number PIN. Your health facility can also register you and give you your PIN.

Riders

Motorbike, tricycle and car owners sign up in the app with their Ghana Card, licence and vehicle details. The zone manager checks and approves riders before they receive any calls. Riders are paid for every completed trip.

Health facilities

Facility staff sign up in the app and are approved by their facility head. Facilities see who is coming, mark ready or redirect, follow up their patients and record health tips in their own voice.

Districts and committees

We set up each district with a zone manager, the local facilities and a rider group. If you would like MAlert in your district, or want to start a Chango fund, write to us.

Questions people ask

Is this an ambulance service?

No. Riders are local people with motorbikes, tricycles and cars, approved by the zone manager. For many women they are the quickest way to reach care. In a life-threatening emergency you can also call 112.

What if no rider accepts?

Riders are called a few at a time, nearest first. If nobody accepts, the zone manager for the district is alerted and can assign a rider directly.

What if the facility is full?

The facility can send her to another facility nearby that is accepting patients. She and her rider are told straight away, and the route changes.

Do I need internet?

Yes, the app needs mobile data to call a rider. If location is off or GPS is weak, she can type where she is or have the rider call her for directions.

I can't read well. Can I still use it?

Yes. Sign-in is a phone number and a 4-number PIN, not a password or code, and many screens have a spoken guide in English, Twi, Ewe, Ga or Dagbanli.

I forgot my PIN.

Tap "Forgot PIN?" to get a code by SMS and choose a new PIN. Or ask your health facility or zone manager to set a new one for you.

Is there an iPhone version?

Not yet. Android comes first because most of the people we work with use Android phones. The app will also be on Google Play soon.

What happens to my information?

Only the people handling an emergency see her details, and only what they need. We do not sell data or show adverts. You can delete your account from the app at any time. Read the privacy policy.
